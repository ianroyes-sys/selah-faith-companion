01

The brand

Selah is the Hebrew word that appears 74 times in the Psalms. Scholars believe it means pause — stop and listen. That is the entire brand in one word: in a feed-driven world engineered to never let you stop, Selah is the app that asks you to.

Mission

Give young Christians a private, unhurried place to meet God every day — one that treats their inner life as sacred, not as data.

Who it's for

Core: Christian women 18–26 — college, first jobs, situationships, group chats, anxiety at 1am. They love God and are tired of performing. Broader: young adults 18–28 who want a faith habit that feels like a journal, not a megachurch.

Positioning

For young believers who feel watched everywhere else, Selah is the faith companion that stays between you and God — journal, prayers, and AI run on your phone, community is anonymous, and nothing is ever sold.

The one-liner

"A quiet space to pray, reflect, and grow — where your prayers never leave your phone."

Logo

The mark is three ideas stacked: a faint arch (sanctuary, doorway), a serif S lit from below like dawn, and a small bronze dot — the pause itself. In the wordmark, the dot becomes a period: Selah. Full stop. Rest here.

Rules
  • Clearspace: keep a margin equal to the height of the pause dot × 4 on all sides.
  • Minimum size: wordmark 90px wide on screen; icon never below 24px.
  • The dot is always bronze #B08347 — in both colorways.
  • Wordmark face is a classic serif (Georgia / New York). Never a sans, never a script.
Never
  • Don't stretch, outline, rotate, or add drop shadows.
  • Don't recolor the S — cream/espresso only.
  • Don't set the wordmark over busy photos without a scrim of espresso at 60%+.
  • Don't pair with emoji or add a cross to the mark — restraint is the brand.

Proportions

60% field (cream, or espresso→night gradient) · 30% warm neutrals (sand, mocha, linen) · 10% bronze. Bronze is jewelry, never wallpaper — one glowing element per composition.

Contrast

Espresso on cream passes AAA — default body pairing. Cream on espresso/night for dark pieces. Bronze on cream is large text and icons only; on espresso it shines at any size.

04

Typography

Two voices: an editorial serif that speaks (headlines, scripture, the wordmark) and a calm sans that serves (body, labels, UI). The serif is the sermon; the sans is the pew.

In-app these are Apple's New York and SF Pro. Outside the app — ads, web, Canva — use Georgia (or Playfair Display / Lora if you want more contrast) with Inter for body.

Setting rules
  • Headlines in sentence case, never all-caps — caps are reserved for small eyebrow labels with wide tracking.
  • No exclamation points. The brand never shouts; it invites.
  • Scripture is always attributed, always in the serif, often italic.
  • Generous line-height (1.6–1.8 body) and whitespace — crowded layouts break the "pause" promise.
05

Voice

Selah sounds like the wisest friend you have — the one who listens all the way to the end, quotes scripture only when it actually fits, and never makes you feel behind. Quiet, warm, honest. Never preachy, never guilt, never hype.

We say

"Put the day down before you put your head down."

"You said your heart feels anxious today. God is not put off by that."

"Someone prayed over what you shared."

"One quiet minute keeps it alive. Come home."

We never say

"Unlock your BEST spiritual life!! 🔥🙏"

"Don't lose your streak!!" (we say it once, gently)

"Crush your quiet time goals."

Guilt of any kind — "You've been away…" framed as failure.

Mechanics
  • Second person, present tense. Short sentences. Periods, not exclamation points.
  • No emojis in product or brand copy. In social captions, at most one, and only if it earns its place.
  • Name real life: exams, group chats, situationships, the 1am scroll — not abstract "spiritual journeys."
  • God is spoken of with reverence and familiarity at once — "He has not forgotten. Neither should you stop asking."
06

Taglines & messaging pillars

Primary tagline: "Faith, at your pace." Alternates for specific placements: "Selah means pause." · "A quiet space to pray, reflect, and grow." · "Your prayers never leave your phone."

Every ad should lean on exactly one of these four pillars:

Pillar 1 · Sacred privacy (the differentiator — lead with this)

Truth behind it: journal, vault, and AI are on-device; community is anonymous; nothing is ever sold. Competitors cannot honestly say this.

"She tells this app things she's never said out loud. It can't tell anyone — the AI lives on her phone."

"Your 1am prayers are not a data point."

Pillar 2 · The pause (anti-scroll, anti-hustle)

Truth behind it: Selah means pause; Morning Reset and Evening Wind-Down are 60-second liturgies written over your actual calendar.

"You've scrolled for 40 minutes. This takes one."

"The only app on your phone that wants less of your time."

Pillar 3 · Companionship without judgment

Truth behind it: the Companion listens first, remembers your walk, answers with scripture — and it's private, so honesty costs nothing.

"Vent, ask, wonder, doubt. Selah listens and answers with scripture, not judgment."

"It remembered what I was praying about. And it asked me how it went."

Pillar 4 · Community without performance

Truth behind it: anonymous prayer wall, circles with real friends, The Commons with rotating handles — belonging with zero social pressure.

"847 people are praying right now. None of them know your name."

"Post the prayer you'd never post on Instagram."

07

Ad & content direction

The look
  • Golden hour and window light. Linen, wood, ceramic mugs, an open journal, hands.
  • Real rooms: dorm desk at 7am, car before work, bed at 11pm. Quiet solitude, not crowds.
  • UI always shown on device frames over cream or espresso — never floating on white.
  • Motion is slow and breathing — fades and gentle scale, like the app's breath circle.
Never
  • Stock worship-concert crowds, stage lights, raised-hands clichés.
  • Neon gradients, glassmorphism rainbows, hype-app energy.
  • Guilt hooks ("God noticed you skipped…") — trust is the product.
  • Overpromising: never "hear from God," never therapy or crisis claims.

Proven-format hooks (TikTok / Reels, 15–30s)

"POV: you replaced your 7am doomscroll with 60 seconds of this." (screen-record Morning Reset)

"An app just asked how my heart was. Not my email. My heart." (check-in flow)

"I posted the prayer I couldn't say out loud — and 30 strangers prayed for me by morning." (wall + 'someone prayed' notification)

"Things I journal into an app that literally cannot leak: …" (privacy promise page close-up)

"My month with God, wrapped." (Faith Wrapped share card — built-in viral loop)

Organic engine

  • Verse cards (the app's 3 share styles) posted daily — the SELAH watermark is the ad.
  • Faith Wrapped screenshots at month-end — prompt users to share theirs.
  • Founder voice: build-in-public + "why your prayers shouldn't be training data" — the privacy story earns earned media.
  • Hashtag base: #christiangirl #faithoverfear #quiettime #prayerjournal #bibleapp #selah
